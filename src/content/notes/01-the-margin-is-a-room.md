## The Change

I did not set out to make the margin a room. I only wanted to stop writing verdicts where questions should go. For years I had marked drafts the way I had been marked: a number at the end, a few sentences in the margin explaining the number, the whole exchange over before the student had a chance to answer. The margin was a place I used, not a place anyone else could occupy. Then I replaced the grades with questions, and the margin stopped being mine.

At first the questions were small and practical — Why here? What does this paragraph want? Who is speaking? — the kind a careful reader asks without thinking. But a question behaves differently from a grade. A grade closes the exchange; a question leaves a door ajar. Students began answering, first in the next draft, then in the margin itself, in smaller handwriting, as if the page had grown a second voice. I had meant to improve the feedback. I had accidentally built a room.

## The Room

The room has no walls but the edge of the page, and it is wide enough for two voices if they stand close. I learned to leave space: to ask fewer questions, to write them smaller, and to let the student have the last word. When I crowded the margin, the conversation stopped. When I left it half empty, the students filled it.

<aside class="sidenote">The margin is the Vygotskian space: the distance between what a writer can do alone and what they can do with a more capable reader.</aside>

What surprised me was not that the correspondence grew, but that it grew past me. Students began writing in one another's margins before I touched the page. The room I had built for two became a room for many, and I took my place in it as one reader among the rest. The grade, when it arrived, felt almost impolite — a number standing at the door of a conversation it had not been invited to join.

## What Stayed

I keep the margin questions because they turned evaluation into a place. The grade has not disappeared; the institution still requires a number. But it no longer gets the first word or the last. The margin does, and the margin is now a room with more than one key.
