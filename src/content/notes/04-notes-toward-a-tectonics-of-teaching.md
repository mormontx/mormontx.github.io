## The Claim

Most writing about teaching is written as if teaching were a recipe: do this, then this, and the room will follow. I have never found it so. A classroom is not a sequence of steps but a structure under load — a ring of chairs, a prompt, a silence, a margin — and like any structure it can be documented only if you name the forces moving through it. This site is an attempt at that documentation. I call it a tectonics because I want the word to do the work: the study of how things stand.

## Loads

Every teaching structure carries loads. Some are epistemic — a question the room must hold without dropping. Some are social — who speaks, who is heard, who is allowed to be slow. Some are the teacher's own: the reflex to rescue, to fill the silence, to grade the draft before it can answer back. A structure is not good because it avoids these loads, but because it names them and stays standing.

<aside class="sidenote">The difference between a tip and a structure: a tip tells you what to do; a structure tells you what is bearing weight and where it will fail.</aside>

## Constraints

A structure is its constraints. The circle works because the teacher may speak only in questions after minute ten. The under-specified prompt works because it may not define its own grading criteria. The timed silence works because no one, including the instructor, may speak during the interval. Strip the constraint and you strip the structure; what remains is a mood.

## Failure Points

I document failure because failure is where a structure becomes legible. The circle fails when two dominant voices become its walls. The prompt fails when one strong reading freezes into canon. The margin fails when the teacher's hand crowds the student's. These are not accidents to be embarrassed by; they are the load tests. A teaching record that omits them is not a record but a portrait.

## The Archive

So I keep these notes the way an engineer keeps a notebook: not to show how well the thing works, but to see where it is working at all. The goal is not tips. The goal is a set of structures a colleague could read, stress, and rebuild for a room I have never entered. Teaching is mostly lost the moment the room empties. This archive is an argument that it need not be.
